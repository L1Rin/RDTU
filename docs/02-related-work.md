# Extended related work

RDTU studies direct numerical text generation with supervised instruction tuning and reinforcement-driven refinement for time series forecasting. In the following, we review existing literature across two key dimensions: traditional Transformer-based approaches and emerging LLM-based methodologies for time series forecasting (TSF).

#### Transformer-based TSF

Deep learning architectures have fundamentally reshaped time series forecasting, with Transformers initially gaining prominence due to their sequence modeling capabilities  [Wen et al., 2022](references.md#wen2022transformers). To address computational bottlenecks, efficient variants such as Informer  [Zhou et al., 2021](references.md#zhou2021informer), Reformer  [Kitaev et al., 2020](references.md#kitaev2020reformer), and Pyraformer  [Liu et al., 2022](references.md#liu2022pyraformer) introduced sparse attention mechanisms to reduce complexity for long-sequence modeling. Subsequently, decomposition-based architectures like Autoformer  [Wu et al., 2021](references.md#wu2021autoformer) and FEDformer  [Zhou et al., 2022](references.md#zhou2022fedformer) enhanced stability on non-stationary data by explicitly disentangling seasonal and trend components. Most recently, PatchTST  [Nie et al., 2023](references.md#nie2022time) and iTransformer  [Liu et al., 2023](references.md#liu2023itransformer) have achieved state-of-the-art results by employing patching techniques and inverted embeddings to effectively capture multivariate correlations and local semantic contexts.

#### LLM-based TSF

The unprecedented capabilities of foundation models have catalyzed cross-modality learning in time series, evolving from the zero-shot textual generation of LLMTime  [Nate Gruver et al., 2023](references.md#gruver2023llmtime) to alignment-based adaptations. Notably, GPT4TS  [Zhou et al., 2023](references.md#zhou2023one) validates freezing LLM backbones with fine-tuned embeddings, while Time-LLM  [Jin et al., 2024](references.md#jin2024timellm) employs a reprogramming framework to align temporal patches with text prototypes, and UniTime  [Liu et al., 2024](references.md#liu2024unitime) utilizes textual instructions for cross-domain unification. Concurrently, research has expanded into visual modalities: VisionTS  [Chen et al., 2024](references.md#chen2024visionts) adapts masked autoencoders for zero-shot forecasting by treating time series as visual signals; Time-VLM  [Zhong et al., 2025](references.md#zhong2025time) leverages vision-language models by converting data into visual plots; and DMMV-A  [Shen et al., 2025](references.md#shen2025dmmv) enhances large vision models through multi-view constraints to optimize long-term forecasting stability. Beyond adapting general-purpose LLMs, recent studies have also explored dedicated time-series foundation models pre-trained on large-scale temporal corpora, such as Chronos-2, TimesFM, Moirai, and MOMENT  [Ansari et al., 2025](references.md#ansari2025chronos2), [Das et al., 2024](references.md#das2024decoder), [Woo et al., 2024](references.md#woo2024unified), [Goswami et al., 2024](references.md#goswami2024moment).
